## 1001 by 1001 grid at q = 1009

Delete a horizontal line. Each affine row or column has 1009 points. Within the selected grid each line has eight private points, 1001 ordinary crossings and one point at infinity. All choices of a deleted line are equivalent under relabelling of the incidence function. Let delta be the cost of a minimum monotone binary circuit before deletion minus the cost after deletion.

| Horizontal infinity input | Vertical infinity input | Delta |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| free | free | between 10 and 1011 |
| 0 | free | at least 11 |
| free | 0 | at least 12 |
| 0 | 0 | exactly 1010 |
| 1 | free | exactly 1 |
| free | 1 | exactly 1011 |

For the zero restrictions, each line still has private points. Apply the active-point plus distinct-neighbourhood lower bound before deletion and the row upper bound after deletion. A remaining hub on 1001 lines costs 999 in the budget. With both hubs removed the row bounds are tight on both sides. The exact affine costs are 2022019 and 2021009. With both hubs present the respective intervals are [2022023,2024021] and [2021013,2023010]. These intervals are bounds on two independent minima.

For horizontal hub = 1, the deleted horizontal contributes only its selector, an independent variable ORed with the surviving function. Pinning the selector off saves at least one gate; appending one OR gate is sufficient. For vertical hub = 1, all column selectors enter as free OR terms. All 1009 affine bits on the deleted horizontal now occur only with its selector. Pin 1008 of them false for one gate apiece. Both phases of the last bit are nonconstant, so expensive_phase gives two more gates. The horizontal selector is still essential in both phases (use the horizontal hub), and pinning it off saves one more. The reverse construction takes 1009 binary ORs, one AND and one output OR. The unrestricted 10 bound uses the same argument with eight private points. These marginal applications are paper arguments. The accompanying script checks multiplicities at full scale and Boolean identities on the 2 by 2 analogue, not minimum circuits by enumeration.

Subtract the exact affine costs from the original minima. The unrestricted delta equals ten exactly when the excess after deletion exceeds the excess before deletion by 1000. It is at least eleven exactly when the excess increases by at most 999. A lower bound of 2023021 before deletion or an upper bound of 2022012 after deletion would already suffice. No such bound or counterexample is established here.

### Restoring the crossings of one row

Keep both infinity bits free. Fix to zero all but k of the 1001 crossing point bits on a chosen horizontal line. Let D(k) and E(k) be the exact minimum costs before and after deleting the line in that restricted function. Permuting the columns shows these costs depend only on k. Other rows, all private bits, and both hubs remain available.

E(k) = E(0)+k: after deletion each restored bit is a twin of an existing private point of its column. One OR suffices to add a twin and pinning an essential bit false saves at least one gate. At k=0 the deleted row has eight private bits and just the horizontal hub in common with other rows. The eight-private-point lower bound saves ten. A row term on nine point bits can be appended in exactly ten gates, so D(0)=E(0)+10.

One restoration increases D by at least one via essentiality, and at most three: append the OR of the row and column selectors, its AND with the restored bit, and an output OR. Thus the unrestricted marginal equals 10 plus the sum over k=1,...,1001 of D(k)-D(k-1)-1. All summands are 0, 1 or 2. A single strict increment at any stage settles the 11-gate target. Exact ten forces every increment to be one. The arithmetic endpoint criterion and a circuit-level reader obstruction are proved in PrivateGridMarginal.lean; their geometric instantiation here is on paper.

In particular, if the original marginal equals ten, choose any crossing and restore it last on its row. Pinning it false then reduces the optimum by exactly one. In every least clean monotone circuit it has exactly one direct reader and that reader is an OR. Two readers, or one AND reader and its live successor, would save at least two. This applies to all 1002001 crossings at once by symmetry. No two of their readers coincide: the two sole inputs to the same OR would be interchangeable, whereas two different crossings have distinct row/column neighbourhoods.

Moreover the other input to each such OR must be zero on every singleton assignment. It is independent of the crossing bit by acyclicity and sole readership. If it fired on a point singleton different from the crossing, select one of the two lines through the crossing missing that point. Toggling the crossing changes the target but cannot change its OR reader. If it fired on a selector singleton, turn on all selectors and no points for the same contradiction. The crossing singleton itself reduces to the all-zero assignment for the partner. Consequently the partner cannot be an OR-only expression of raw inputs. It must also fire on some incident point-selector pair without that crossing: otherwise bypassing the OR by the raw crossing preserves every minimal true assignment and can only decrease the computed function, contradicting minimality. These conditions do not imply distinct partner wires.

There must also be an AND downstream of each crossing reader, since an OR-only path to the output would accept the crossing singleton. Thus circuits with at most one AND per input-output path cannot witness exact ten. This leaves general circuits with nested ANDs.

### Shared partners with both infinity inputs free

Suppose every restoration costs one. For a partner wire T, let G be the set of crossings whose unique direct reader is OR(x,T). Call G spanning if it touches every horizontal and vertical selector. If T is true, increasing any x in G to one leaves its reader unchanged, and leaves the entire downstream evaluation unchanged. Saturating G therefore preserves the answer. Since every selected line has an incident saturated point, the answer on T=true equals Y, the OR of all 2002 selectors. In particular T AND Y is a sound accepting signal. If T already implies the output, Y can be omitted.

Bypass all |G| readers, feeding each crossing bit directly to the former reader's consumers. Keep T if needed. The bypassed output is smaller pointwise by monotonicity. On T=false the outputs coincide; on T=true restore the correct answer with a final OR of T AND Y. This costs at most 2001 OR gates for Y, one AND and one output OR. Hence a spanning partner in a least circuit has |G| <= 2003. If T implies the target (for instance, a raw incident point-selector conjunction), two shared readers already contradict minimality: bypass them and append one OR with T.

For r distinct spanning partners bypass all M affected readers simultaneously. Their computed signals T'_i in the modified DAG can decrease. Repair with output OR ((OR_i T'_i) AND Y). This costs 2002+r gates. It is sound because T'_i implies T_i. For completeness, on a true input with a lost output, take the earliest bypassed reader whose value changed in topological order. Its partner still has its original true value at that position (each preceding gate value agrees, including all wires of the partner computation). Thus OR_i T'_i and Y are both true. Consequently M <= 2002+r. Each spanning group has at least 1001 crossings. For r >= 3 the bound fails. At most two distinct direct partner wires can each span all lines in a tight circuit.

A related sufficient condition for indirect sharing: if truth of an internal wire U forces all partners of a spanning family to be true, U AND Y is sound by the same saturation argument (increasing an affected input when its sole OR reader is already true cannot change any downstream values). Cut U to false in its consumers and constant-fold. Where U was false the cut agrees; where U was true, U AND Y restores the correct output. Preserve the upstream circuit for U as a tap. If more than 2003 gates downstream of U disappear, this improves the circuit. A common AND ancestor followed by other AND gates need not imply the descendant partners.

The arithmetic gives actual obstructions but does not rule out nonspanning sharing: grouping crossings by horizontal row gives 1001 groups of 1001, each missing 1000 horizontal selectors. This is only a pattern of direct uses, not a circuit implementing the function. No conclusion about optimality follows from the pattern. The semantic single-partner identity and saturation identity are Lean checked. The DAG rewiring, simultaneous-group count and application to the concrete grid above are paper arguments.

### Row-local partners and a common AND control

Assume the tight marginal and additionally that, for each row i, all its 1001 direct crossing readers have the form OR(x_ij,T_i). Different rows may use the same partner. The conclusions below are conditional on this row-wise architecture.

First one can retain only one of the eight private points of each selected line. Every pin of a redundant private input to zero reduces the minimum by exactly one: pinning an essential variable saves one, and the old function is recovered by one OR into its surviving twin. In any least clean circuit one may constant-fold the pin. At least the direct reader of the private bit disappears. It is not a crossing reader, since the other input of a sole crossing OR cannot be true at a singleton. No second gate can disappear, by exactness. In particular the crossing readers survive and the row-wise common wire is preserved, possibly as an alias. Reapply the singleton test in the restricted incidence function: each crossing still has its two distinct incident lines, and every other point misses at least one of them. Thus the compressed optimum obtained by these folds retains the assumed row architecture. Paths in it lift to paths of the original DAG by undoing the wire bypasses.

Choose any r distinct rows, and bypass all 1001r of their crossing ORs. Write V for the OR of all vertical selectors, and T'_i for the partner taps after the bypass. The term V AND (OR_i T'_i) is sound: if T_i is true, raising any crossing in its row preserves the evaluation, so any active column gives a true incidence. Monotonicity gives T'_i <= T_i. A lost positive minimal input (an incident point and selector pair) has an earliest changed bypassed reader. Its modified partner is still true because all preceding wires agree. This handles every lost pair with a vertical selector.

To handle horizontal pairs, form a row construction with the one private point and the horizontal infinity point on every row. Also include each crossing bit with a path to any selected partner, on its own horizontal row. Let a be the number of such distinct crossing bits. This is sound, and covers every remaining lost pair: on a crossing paired with a horizontal selector, the earliest changed partner was true, but false on the selector singleton, so the crossing has a path to it. Monotonicity of the repaired circuit extends coverage of the minimal pairs to all positive inputs. The horizontal construction costs 3002+a (2002+a incidences plus 1001 rows minus one). Counting the 1000 gates for V, r-1 to combine the taps, one AND and two output ORs gives **4004+r+a**. Hence least size requires 1001r <= 4004+r+a for every nonempty row subset. Keeping the original eight private inputs instead gives the independent check 11011+r+a.

Put an edge i -> j when any crossing of row i reaches T_j. This strictly orders the partners topologically since T_i feeds that crossing reader. In a prefix consisting of entire dependency-depth layers, let w be the last layer width. No crossing from the last layer or from outside the prefix reaches a prefix partner, so a <= 1001(r-w). Thus w <= 4 in each proper prefix, and w <= 5 in the last layer. Covering 1001 rows takes at least 250 layers. Their dependency chain gives a circuit path with at least 249 intervening AND gates: a path from a crossing reader to the next partner consisting solely of ORs would make that partner true on the crossing singleton. The original-eight calculation gives at least 91 layers even without compression. This rules out parallel row-local sharing and shallow inter-row sharing, but not an arbitrarily deep circuit.

If H is an ancestor of every T_i (allowing equality), H cannot depend on any crossing, since the crossing's only reader follows its own partner. If also T_i implies H for every row (as in T_i = H AND U_i), let h count the horizontal selector singleton inputs on which H is true. For the all-row bypass the horizontal correction can use just crossings on those h rows: H on a crossing-horizontal pair has the same value as on the selector singleton. The repair bound is then 1001^2 <= 4004+1001+1001h, or h >= 996. Under private-point compression the common signal can be retained as a tap or its folded alias; if it folds to constant false the crossing partners cannot be live. This test rules out common enables requiring a point bit. A common signal with sufficiently broad horizontal singleton support and deep descendant logic is still open.

The row-layer arithmetic and a separate common-control repair identity were checked in Lean. The grid-specific constant folding, gate count, and DAG conclusions above are paper arguments.

### Dark-column repair rules out the entire row-wise architecture

Use the original eight private points per line. Assume exact ten, hence each crossing has a distinct sole OR reader. Group the crossings by the actual other input wire T of that reader; write g(T) for group size. Pick a vertical line c. Pin its 1009 affine point bits and the vertical infinity bit to zero. If a group touches c, its partner now implies the restricted incidence function. Indeed, on a purported negative input with T true, turn on selector c. The output is still false since c has no active point, and T stays true. Turn on the group's crossing on c in the ORIGINAL circuit: its sole reader is already true, so the output cannot change; the selected incidence requires it to be true. This argument uses no depth or independence assumption between partners.

Bypass all g(T) crossing readers of all the groups touching c on the pinned input. Append the OR of their modified partner taps to the output (one binary OR per group). Each tap can only decrease and is sound. A lost positive has a first changed reader in topological order; its partner is still true there. This recovers exactly the pinned function, even for cascaded partners. Restore the original unrestricted function with at most 4014 more gates:

* 1002 to append the vertical infinity bit AND the OR of all column selectors;
* 1010 to append selector c AND the OR of its 1009 affine bits;
* 2002 to append the 1001 products of a restored crossing and its horizontal selector.

The final circuit again has both infinity inputs free. Rewiring with zero constants is a construction shorthand: propagate constants/aliases in topological order and prune; the pinned variable ports are available for the restoration terms. Dummy or dead wires only improve the bound. Every minimum circuit in a putative counterexample must therefore satisfy sum_{T touching c}(g(T)-1) <= 4014 for each c, and likewise for each row. If one partner serves every crossing of each row, every group touches c, there are at most 1001 groups, and the left side is at least 1001000. This contradicts minimality regardless of depth, including shared ancestors and chains exceeding 250 layers. The conclusion excludes that architecture in a counterexample; it does not assert that some optimum has that architecture.

For arbitrary groups, put M=1002001, K=number of partners, and r_T,c_T=number of rows/columns touched. Summing both cut inequalities gives sum (g-1)(r_T+c_T) <= 2*4014*1001. As g <= r_T*c_T, the left side is at least 2 sum (g-1)sqrt(g). Jensen for g^(3/2) and Cauchy for sqrt(g) give sqrt(M/K)-sqrt(K/M) <= 4014/1001, hence K >= 55591. For groups each confined to one row the column inequalities alone give sum g(g-1) <= 4014*1001; Cauchy gives K >= 200001. No independence of these wires or their ancestors is required. Different wires may implement identical Boolean functions.

An independent restriction on distinct partners comes from minimal positive pairs. Work in the compressed optimum with one private bit per line, obtained by exact duplicate removal as above (sole crossing ORs persist). For each crossing j and each of its two selectors l, put a directed edge labelled (j,l) from j to crossing i if T_i fires on precisely that input assignment (all other bits off). An edge entails an actual path: T_i is false on the selector singleton and j only feeds its crossing reader. This directed graph is acyclic. For any m selected crossing readers bypass them. Restore the sound predicate consisting of all noncrossing incidences and those crossing-selector pairs which label an incoming edge of the selection. This covers every lost minimal pair by the first changed reader argument. With e distinct incoming labels it costs at most 6006+e additional gates: 4004 noncrossing incidences plus e, 2002 line stars, and one final output OR. Consequently e >= m-6006. Globally at least 995995 distinct labels fire. If m_h denotes the cumulative number of vertices in the first h dependency layers, m_h <= 6006 + 2*m_(h-1), m_0=0. Seven layers cover at most 762762 vertices, so at least eight semantic layers are needed. If no crossing sends both selector labels then m_h <= 6006+m_(h-1), requiring at least 167 layers. These estimates still allow genuinely different, interacting partners.

The Lean lemmas dark_line_sound and restore_column verify the key propositional identities; pair_layer_capacity checks the arithmetic recurrence. The DAG transformation, cost count, concrete grid instantiation, and partner-count estimates are paper arguments.

### Distinct partners: activation, actual loss, and a simultaneous matching

There is a correct circuit family with all the numerical activation requirements and an arbitrarily long dependency chain. Write h,v for the two hub bits, Y for the OR of all line selectors, U for the incidence predicate on all noncrossing points, and L_j for the OR of the two selectors incident to crossing j. Enumerate all M=1002001 crossings. Use T_1=h AND (v OR firstHorizontalSelector). At crossing j, use a sole reader R_j=x_j OR T_j and advance T_(j+1)=T_j OR (R_j AND L_j). Output U OR (T_(M+1) AND Y). The seed consists of the both-hubs mask and one already valid edge. When both hubs are on, any selected line yields an incidence; when no line is selected the guard suppresses the mask. By induction the prefix is the seed OR the earlier crossing incidences. The M partner functions are distinct: successive prefixes differ on either incident pair of the newly added crossing, and the prefixes are nested. Every partner fires on the negative input with both hubs on and no selected line. Both selector labels of every crossing except the last activate a later partner, yielding 2004000 distinct activation labels. All 16384 assignments of a two-by-two instance are checked by tools/check-grid-prefix.py. At full scale these counts follow symbolically.

All M readers can nevertheless be removed: T OR ((x OR T) AND L) equals T OR (x AND L). This works simultaneously by induction and saves one gate per step. More generally, if every path from a reader to the output passes through a join and T reaches that join by an OR-only path independent of the bypassed reader, then bypassing the reader cannot change the output. On T=false it already equals x; on T=true the join remains true. This excludes reconverging uses even for an unsound partner. It gives no bound for a circuit where the indicated paths do not exist.

A stronger test for arbitrary circuits measures actual false negatives. In the tight-marginal hypothesis work with an optimum compressed to one private bit per line as in the preceding sections. Bypass any set S of crossing OR readers; the residual is monotone and at most the target. Let E(S) be the incident point-selector pairs that it rejects on the assignment consisting only of that pair. Adding precisely those edge terms restores the target on every input, by monotonicity. Group them by line. For a nonempty missed edge set the appended repair costs |E(S)| plus its number of used lines (at most 2002), including its join with the residual; for the empty set no gates are needed. Minimality implies |S| <= |E(S)|+2002. The 4004 noncrossing edges account for at most 4004 misses. Hence bypassing all readers must lose at least 995995 distinct crossing-selector pairs, even with both hub variables available to the repaired function. The prefix example loses zero.

This strengthens to a simultaneous assignment. Let B be the crossing-selector pairs LOST after bypassing all readers. For each reader i take W_i to be the pairs in B on which its original partner fires. If a crossing pair is lost after bypassing S, it also belongs to B by monotonicity of bypasses. At its earliest changed reader in S, the original partner was true (and the raw crossing false). Thus |S| <= 6006 + |union of W_i over S| for every S. Add 6006 shared dummy labels and apply Hall's theorem. At least M-6006=995995 different readers can each be assigned a DIFFERENT truly lost crossing-selector pair activating its own partner. Since at most 6006 readers receive dummies, at least 55591-6006=49585 distinct partner wires take part on this same compressed circuit (the dark-cut restoration cost there is at most 4014 as well). The matched source crossing must precede its assigned reader, since the partner vanishes on the selector singleton and the source has a sole reader. Each crossing supplies at most two labels, yielding a directed forest with at most 6006 roots and at most two children per vertex. These are necessary conditions; no impossibility for distributed loss or minimum-size construction realizing it has been established.

Lean checks the prefix identities, the masked-join identity, the repair by genuinely lost edges, and the Hall assignment given the cut inequalities. The grid circuit rewiring and its numerical gate counts are paper arguments.

### A circuit that passes even the matched-loss test

For row i with selector y, define enable=(y OR horizontalHub) AND (y OR verticalHub), equivalent to y OR (both hubs). Start its carry T_1=horizontalHub AND (verticalHub OR y). At column j use R_j=x_ij OR T_j as the only crossing reader; emit R_j AND columnSelector_j to the output OR and set T_(j+1)=R_j AND enable. After the last column emit T_(1002) AND y. Also emit the noncrossing incidence predicate U. If both hubs are on, U already accepts any selected line and the emitted terms require a selected line. Otherwise enable=y and the carry records an earlier horizontal incidence (including the seed); any extra vertical term caused by a true carry is therefore sound. This computes the full unrestricted function. All partner signals fire on the negative input with both hubs on and all selectors off. Within a row their functions differ on newly added horizontal crossing incidences; different rows are distinguished by their horizontal selector and horizontal hub alone.

After bypassing all readers by their raw bits, the only crossing contribution on a horizontal-only pair is from the last column in its row. All vertical pairs survive. Thus exactly 1001*1000=1001000 horizontal crossing-selector pairs are actually lost. Assign each such pair (i,j) to the reader (i,j+1): its original partner fires, with distinct labels and recipients. There are M distinct partners. This passes even the joint numerical and matching conditions; it is NOT a minimum circuit. The drawn readers, carry-update ANDs, and vertical emission ANDs alone use 3M=3006003 gates, exceeding the unrestricted row-construction upper bound 2024021 before counting the output aggregation. The exhaustive two-by-two script also verifies the second circuit and its exact lost minimal pairs.

One size constraint on a putative tight optimum clarifies the gap. In the original grid the post-deletion row upper bound is 2023010; adding the hypothesized ten gives a pre-deletion upper bound of 2023020. Compressing seven duplicates on each of 2002 lines removes exactly 14014 gates, leaving at most 2009006. Its M crossing readers are distinct from all K partners: readers accept a crossing singleton while partners reject every singleton. At most 1007005-K gates can lie outside these two sets. If K=M, this is just 5004 gates. This constraint concerns the compressed optimum and permits arbitrary sharing within the two sets. It does not imply that the carry construction's three separate gate families are necessary in general.

The all-distinct case is excluded by the [first-consumer probe bound](grid-first-consumer.md).
