# First consumers of the crossing readers

In the 1001 by 1001 grid, the all-distinct-partner case cannot occur in a minimum circuit under the ten-gate hypothesis. The proof uses two selector tests per crossing. It counts gates already used as partners at full value and permits unrestricted fanout and arbitrary depth.

## General bound

Write M for the number of crossings, K for the number of distinct partner wires, and B for the number of gates outside the crossing readers and their partners. Assume each crossing input x_i has the sole reader R_i=x_i OR T_i. Its endpoints are a horizontal and a vertical selector. No two crossings have the same endpoint pair. The output is the incidence function. Use a clean binary monotone circuit, pruning constants, unused gates and irrelevant raw inputs. Every R_i is distinct (sharing a reader would make the two raw bits interchangeable). T_i is independent of x_i by acyclicity and sole readership.

Every partner is false on singleton inputs. On a selector singleton, otherwise turn on all selectors and toggle its crossing, changing the target but not its reader. On another point singleton, turn on an endpoint of its crossing missing the other point, and toggle its crossing for the same contradiction. On its own point singleton use independence. Constant false partners are redundant in a minimum circuit. Thus partners are internal gates, distinct from all readers (R_i accepts its own point singleton).

In fact every partner is false on EVERY nonincident input pair consisting of a point p and a selected line l. For the partner of x_z with p different from z, turn on another endpoint b of z missing p (it may already be selected). If T_z were true, it would remain true. There is still no incidence, but raising x_z now creates one without changing its reader. For p=z use independence and rejection of selector singletons. This applies equally in the affine grid with both infinity inputs present and after compressing private twins.

For each i make two probes. In each compare the selector singleton l with the same selector plus x_i, for l one of its endpoints. The output changes. R_i is the only raw reader, and cannot itself be the output since it accepts x_i alone. Charge the probe to the earliest changed gate other than R_i. It must consume R_i. Its other input has its old value: all earlier gates other than R_i agree, and the only changed raw bit cannot directly feed this gate. Duplicate gate inputs are excluded in a minimum clean circuit, or simplify the duplicate first. Another crossing reader cannot be the charged gate: its operands are a raw crossing and a partner, neither of which is R_i.

* An outside gate that consumes a crossing reader receives at most four charges (two inputs, two probes per crossing). Let O count all such outside gates.
* A charged partner must be AND: an OR consuming R_i would accept x_i alone. Its other wire W is true on the tested selector singleton, since the AND turns on and W is unchanged. W cannot be another crossing reader since all readers are false on selector singletons. Thus this partner receives charges from only one crossing, at most two in total.
* When a partner receives both charges, W is true on exactly the two endpoint selector singletons. For any other selector l, W true at l would stay true on (x_i,l), and R_i would be true there, so the AND partner would accept a nonincident pair. This is impossible by the preceding nonincidence argument.

Count the distinct two-selector signatures needed by these exceptional charges. Several doubly charged partners cannot be charged by the same crossing, because each probe chooses exactly one gate. Their endpoint pairs are therefore distinct. For each signature take its earliest wire in the DAG, including the raw inputs. It cannot be raw (a raw selector has a singleton signature, a raw point the empty signature). It cannot be a reader or a partner (both have empty selector-singleton signature). An outside gate consuming a reader cannot create that signature for the first time either: on selector singletons an AND with a reader is always false, and an OR copies its other operand. So each exceptional charge has a different origin among the B-O outside gates not consuming readers. If S is the number of doubly charged partners, S+O <= B.

Consequently 2M <= K+S+4O <= K+4B. This accounts for reuse of partners as gates, direct use of other readers, and reuse of selector signals. It places no restriction on how signals are computed on inputs with several selectors.

## Order 1009

M=1002001. If K=M, then B >= ceil(M/4)=250501. The required total size is at least 2254503. This exceeds even the unconditional original row-construction upper bound 2024021. In particular, no optimum with all-distinct partners and sole OR readers can realize exact ten. Under the ten-gate hypothesis the compressed size is at most 2009006, leaving 5004 outside gates if K=M. The original uncompressed ten-gate budget instead leaves 19018; this independent contradiction avoids assuming that distinct partners remain distinct when private inputs are pinned.

With arbitrary sharing the same bound gives K <= 674672 in the compressed tight optimum. Combined with the dark-column lower bound the remaining necessary range there is 55591 through 674672. In the uncompressed tight optimum alone the upper bound is 693358. These ceilings do not decide existence in the remaining interval. The earlier restoration argument shows that an overall counterexample requires sole OR readers for all crossings in every optimum. The all-distinct analysis excludes this architecture from any such counterexample; it does not assume that there exists a minimum with all-distinct partners.

## Restoring several dark columns via private inputs

Work in the compressed tight optimum with one private point per line and both infinity bits initially free. Choose d of the n=1001 columns. Temporarily set their affine points and the vertical infinity bit to zero. Every partner whose group touches a chosen column is sound on the restricted function by the dark-column argument. Bypass all crossing readers of those partners and append their modified taps to the output. The earliest changed reader argument applies without any ordering assumption on the groups. This saves at least the sum of g-1 over touched partners, where g is their group size.

Restore the original function by precomposing the repaired restricted circuit with the following substitutions. OR the d removed crossings of each row into its own remaining private point (nd gates). OR the vertical infinity bit into the private point of each surviving column (n-d gates). Each of these private bits has just its own line as neighbour, so the substitutions restore exactly the appropriate incidences, even if the restricted circuit uses the bit in many places. For each of the d missing columns append its full line term. There are n crossings, one private point and one infinity bit on that line, so the term and its output OR cost n+3 gates. Total: nd+(n-d)+(n+3)d=2004d+1001. The appendices compute the original unrestricted function. Thus the sum saved cannot exceed this restoration cost. The transposed bound holds for row cuts.

For d=1 this costs 3005, and the integer inequality 29g <= 117+3(g-1)(r+c), where g<=rc and r,c are the numbers of occupied rows and columns, already implies K>=94103. A stronger certificate uses d=24, cost 49097. For a group touching c columns keep any t=min(c,7) of them. In a uniform 24-subset each retained column occurs with probability 24/n and two specified columns occur together with probability 24*23/[n(n-1)]. The first two inclusion-exclusion terms therefore lower-bound its hit probability by 24*s(c)/(2000n), where s(c)=2000t-23t(t-1). Apply the same inequality to a uniform row cut. Upon summing over all groups and taking expectations of the two cut bounds, obtain
24 sum (g-1)(s(r)+s(c)) <= 2*2000*n*49097.
No independence between the groups is needed.

For every nonempty group, 12678g <= 26988+(g-1)(s(r)+s(c)). When either dimension is at least seven its capped score 13034 suffices; the remaining dimensions range from one through six and g<=rc. The inequality is sharp on complete 2 by 2 and 2 by 3 rectangles. Summing it and using sum g=n^2 gives **K>=167199**. Together with the first-consumer bound the compressed tight optimum requires **167199 <= K <= 674672**. This is still a necessary condition. The Lean file checks the private-bit identity for a single restored column, the integer group inequalities and final numerical implications. The circuit transformation and averaging argument here are on paper.

## Counting the gates immediately after the readers in the dark cut

The following bounds are for the same private-point-compressed tight optimum. Let a consumer be any gate directly reading a crossing reader R_i. A reader cannot be the output (it accepts its crossing singleton), and in a least circuit it has a consumer. If it has just one consumer, that consumer cannot take another reader R_j as its other operand. For an AND, selecting an endpoint of i and then enabling x_i cannot affect the output: R_j is initially false and is upstream of the sole consumer, so independent of x_i. For an OR choose an endpoint of i missing j. Turn on x_j and this selector; the target is false. Adding x_i makes the target true, but x_i is read only by R_i, whose only consumer is already true because R_j is true. These contradictions also cover sharing between partners further downstream. No crossing reader is itself a consumer of another crossing reader.

There is an injective choice of a consumer for every crossing reader. Readers of fanout one have exclusive consumers by the previous paragraph. In any selected subset the remaining readers have at least two incidences each, and no consumer has more than two reader inputs. Their neighbours thus number at least as many as these remaining readers, disjoint from the exclusive consumers. This is Hall's condition.

When d columns are dark, bypass the touched groups as before. For each of the nd dark crossings the bypass substitutes a constant zero for its reader. Its matched consumer becomes a constant or an alias even if the other operand also changes. Remove all these distinct consumer gates by topological constant folding, redirecting any partner taps as well. They are separate from the crossing readers already counted. Thus the net allowance for the sum of g-1 over touched groups is only (2004d+1001)-1001d=1003d+1001. This works identically for row cuts and restores both unrestricted infinity inputs.

For 24 lines the net allowance is 25073. With the preceding capped score use 8885g <= 11816+(g-1)(s(r)+s(c)), yielding K>=399439. For 47 lines the allowance is 48142. The score is now s(x)=2000t-46t(t-1), t=min(x,7), and 8770g <= 11632+(g-1)(s(r)+s(c)). Averaging gives 47 sum (g-1)(s(r)+s(c)) <= 2*2000*1001*48142, whence K>=402877. The 1 by 2 and 2 by 2 complete groups are equality cases in both supporting inequalities.

## Unused inputs in the first-consumer count

Partition the B outside gates into A, C and N according as they have two, one or zero reader inputs (letters here denote counts). Write p,a,c for the probe charges to partners, A and C. Their sum is 2M. If P0 partners get no charge, the earlier signature-origin argument gives p <= K-P0+S with S<=N. If C0 one-reader outside gates get no charge then c <= 2(C-C0).

At the 2A reader input positions let t positions receive both probes from their reader and let u receive neither. Thus a=2A+t-u and a<=4A. Each double-charged position belongs to a different reader and needs a second consumer by the exclusive-consumer argument. The reader sent both probes to the first gate. Its second consumer supplies either an unused position among the A gates, a C0 gate, or a slot of an uncharged partner. Indeed, a charged partner is an AND whose other operand is true on a selector singleton, so it cannot read a second reader. Counting these distinct positions gives t<=u+C0+2P0. Consequently a<=2A+C0+2P0. Add this bound and a<=4A, use the preceding limits on p and c, and obtain 2M<=K+3B. The positive contribution of P0 cancels. This argument allows gates and wires to be reused throughout the circuit.

Since M+K+B<=2009006, the new upper endpoint is K<=508506. The joint necessary interval is **402877 <= K <= 508506**. Neither endpoint asserts a realizable minimum circuit.
